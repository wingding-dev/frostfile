Never Mailed Anything Like This? Here's Where to Actually Go

🛒 Stamps Any post office — or the checkout at most grocery stores and pharmacies sells a book of stamps (just ask the cashier). One first-class stamp per normal envelope; a fat envelope may need two, and the post office counter will weigh it for you.
🖨️ Photocopies Your public library (usually cheapest), a FedEx Office or UPS Store (FedEx Office is what Kinko's became), or an office-supply store. Many home printers copy too — and a clear phone photo printed out is usually accepted as a “copy.”
📜 Birth certificate Certified copies come from the vital records office of the county or state where the child was born — search “[that county] vital records birth certificate”. There's a fee, and many offices let you order online or by mail. Remember: you mail a photocopy, never the certified original.
💳 Social Security card Lost it? Replacements are free from Social Security — through a “my Social Security” account at ssa.gov, or at a local Social Security office. Again, you only ever mail a photocopy.
✉️ Certified mail Ask for “certified mail with return receipt” at any post office counter — it costs a few dollars extra and gives you proof it arrived. Worth it for envelopes carrying a child's documents.
