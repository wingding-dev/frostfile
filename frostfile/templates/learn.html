No. 1: Freezing Your Credit Does NOT Freeze Your Credit Cards

A credit freeze does not touch anything you already have. Your credit cards keep working. Your car loan, your mortgage, your bank account — all exactly the same as before. Your paycheck still lands. Your debit card still swipes. And your credit score is completely unaffected{{ cite(citer, refs('cfpb-freeze')) }}.

What a freeze stops is someone opening a new account in your name. Before approving new credit, a lender asks a credit bureau — a company that keeps a file about your borrowing — to see your file. A freeze tells the bureau: don't show it. No file check, no new account{{ cite(citer, refs('cfpb-freeze')) }}.

What a Credit Freeze Actually Is

Three big companies — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — are called credit bureaus. Each keeps a file about you: what loans you have, whether you pay on time. You never signed up for this; they keep the file anyway.

A freeze (the law calls it a “security freeze”) tells each bureau: do not release my file to anyone trying to open something new. It's free at all three, and you place it at each one separately. Asked online or by phone, the bureau must freeze your file within one business day{{ cite(citer, refs('cfpb-freeze', 'fcra-1681c1')) }}.

Unfreezing (“Thawing”) — What It Takes

You lift your freeze when someone must check your file to say yes to you: a new loan or card, and often a phone plan or an apartment{{ cite(citer, refs('cfpb-companies-list')) }}. Lifting is free, every time, forever. Ask online or by phone and the bureau must lift it within one hour; you can lift it for just a window of time — a week while the car dealer runs your application — and it refreezes on its own{{ cite(citer, refs('cfpb-freeze', 'fcra-1681c1')) }}.

The one habit that matters: each bureau gives you a PIN or account password when you freeze. That's your key — record it in FrostFile (it's encrypted) and you'll never be stuck. Tip: before applying for anything, ask which bureau the company checks, and thaw only that one.

Why Children Are Prime Targets

A child usually has no credit file at all — exactly what a thief wants. In the government's words, thieves like children's Social Security numbers “because children have no credit blemishes and because the fraudulent activity may go unchecked for years”{{ cite(citer, refs('cfpb-child-report')) }}. Nobody runs a credit check on a 7-year-old, so the fraud sits and grows until they turn 18 and get denied their first loan for debts they never knew existed.

The fix: federal law lets a parent freeze a child under 16 — and if the child has no file, the bureau creates one just to freeze it shut{{ cite(citer, refs('fcra-1681c1', 'cfpb-blog-2018')) }}. It's done by mail with document copies, and FrostFile prints those packets for you. At 16–17 the process changes — the teen requests their own freeze by phone or mail{{ cite(citer, refs('equifax-child-faq', 'ftc-minors-under-16')) }} — and FrostFile switches to teen letters automatically.

Why There Are More Than 3 Places to Freeze

The big three aren't the only companies keeping files on your family. The government maintains a list of dozens of “specialty” reporting companies tracking narrower slices of your life — opening bank accounts, phone and utility service, apartment rentals, payday-style lending{{ cite(citer, refs('cfpb-companies-list')) }}. Freezing the big three does nothing at these — each keeps its own file, so each needs its own freeze. That's why FrostFile's grid is longer than three rows: a locked front door doesn't help if the side door's open.

“Is This Legal? Why Is It Free?”

Completely legal — it's your federal right, and Congress is why it's free. After the giant Equifax breach, Congress passed the Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act (2018), whose Section 301 is literally titled “Protecting consumers' credit”{{ cite(citer, refs('plaw-115-174')) }}. Since September 21, 2018, placing a freeze, lifting it, and re-placing it are free at every nationwide bureau, in every state{{ cite(citer, refs('cfpb-blog-2018', 'fcra-1681c1')) }}. This is not a loophole or a trick — it's a right you already own. FrostFile just helps you use it.
