Where Your Data Lives

Everything FrostFile knows sits in one folder on this computer — a single database file, plus backups. It goes nowhere unless you copy it. Every sensitive value inside — names, Social Security numbers, PINs, reminders, report contents — is scrambled and unreadable without your passphrase; only structural bits (how many adults and children, which freezes are done and when) stay readable so the app can sort and count.

Moving to a New Computer?

One click packages everything — every person, freeze, PIN, and note — into a single scrambled file on your Desktop. Carry that file to the new computer however you like (USB stick works fine), install FrostFile there, and choose “Moving from another computer?” on its first screen. Your passphrase stays the same. Old backups don't need to come along — the new computer starts making its own.

Auto-Lock

When FrostFile sits untouched for this long, it locks itself and asks for the passphrase again — so a walk away from the desk does not leave Social Security numbers on the screen for whoever sits down next.

Backup

Makes a safe copy of your data while the app is running. The copy is scrambled with the same passphrase — fine to put on a USB stick or in cloud storage, and useless to anyone who doesn't have your passphrase. It is also useless to you without it, so make sure the passphrase is written down somewhere safe first.

FrostFile also backs itself up about once a week, when you unlock. It keeps the ten most recent automatic copies and never deletes ones you made with the button above.

Change Master Passphrase

Everything gets re-scrambled under the new passphrase in one step — it either fully succeeds or changes nothing, so a crash partway through cannot ruin your data. Backups you made earlier still open with the old passphrase, so keep it until you have made a fresh backup.

What This App Sends Over the Internet

FrostFile is a program on this computer, not a website or a service. It reaches the internet only when you press one of these buttons — and never on its own:

  • Breaches → Check a password: five characters of a scrambled version of the password. Never the password itself.
  • Breaches → Check an email (and “Check the whole family”): the email address, sent to Have I Been Pwned. Only works once you add your own key.
  • Settings → Save an API key: the key, sent to Have I Been Pwned once to confirm it is valid.
  • Sources → Check All Links Now: visits each agency and government page to see if it still exists. Sends nothing about you — just ordinary page requests.
  • Nothing else. No tracking, no analytics, no update checks, no accounts.

Links out to agency websites open in a new tab with the referrer suppressed, so those sites do not learn you came from a tool like this.
