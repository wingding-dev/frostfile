## 1. Freezing your credit does NOT freeze your credit cards

**The big misunderstanding, cleared up first.**

A credit freeze does not touch anything you already have. Your credit cards
keep working. Your car loan, your mortgage, your bank account — all exactly
the same as before. Your paycheck still lands. Your debit card still swipes.

Here is what a freeze actually stops: someone opening a **new** account in
your name. Before a lender approves a new loan or card, it asks a credit
bureau (a company that keeps a file about your borrowing) to see your file.
A freeze tells the bureau: don't show it. No file, no new account — for a
thief, and for you, until you unlock it [cfpb-freeze].

The law spells out that a freeze does not apply to companies you already have
an account with. They can still see your file to manage your account, collect
a payment, or even raise your credit limit [fcra-1681c1].

And your credit score? Untouched. In the government's own words: "Security
freezes do not impact your credit scores in any way whatsoever" [cfpb-freeze].

**Citations:** [cfpb-freeze], [fcra-1681c1]

**Diagram concept:** A wall with two doors — the "existing accounts" door wide
open with your cards and paycheck flowing through, the "new accounts" door
bolted shut with a thief standing outside.

## 2. What a credit freeze actually is

Three big companies — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — are called credit
bureaus. Each one keeps a file about you: what loans you have, whether you pay
on time. You never signed up for this. They keep the file anyway.

When you apply for a new loan or card, the lender asks a bureau for your file
before saying yes. That check is the front door to your credit.

A freeze (the law calls it a "security freeze") is you telling each bureau:
do not release my file to anyone trying to open something new. Lenders
almost never approve credit they can't check. So when a fraudster applies in
your name, the lender sees a locked file and says no [cfpb-freeze].

Placing a freeze is free at all three bureaus, and you must do it at each one
separately. Ask online or by phone and the bureau must freeze your file within
one business day; by mail, within three [cfpb-freeze] [fcra-1681c1].

**Citations:** [cfpb-freeze], [fcra-1681c1]

**Diagram concept:** Three filing cabinets labeled Equifax, Experian,
TransUnion, each with its own padlock, and a fraudster's loan application
bouncing off all three.

## 3. Freeze vs. fraud alert vs. "lock" vs. monitoring

Four tools with similar names. They are not the same.

**Freeze** — the strongest. Free, backed by federal law. Blocks new-account
checks on your file until you unlock it [cfpb-freeze] [fcra-1681c1].

**Fraud alert** — a warning flag on your file. Lenders must take extra steps
to confirm it's really you before opening new credit. Free, lasts one year
(seven years if you're a proven identity-theft victim). Weaker: your file
stays visible. One nice part — tell one bureau and it must tell the other
two [cfpb-idtheft].

**Credit lock** — the bureaus' own app-based product. It holds your file too,
but it's a company offering, sometimes with a monthly fee, governed by the
company's terms — not by the freeze law. The government says locks are "no
more effective than security freezes" [cfpb-freeze].

**Monitoring** — a paid watch service. It does not block anything. In the
government's words, monitoring services "merely alert you after" your
information has been used [cfpb-monitoring].

Think of it this way: a freeze is a deadbolt on the door. Monitoring is a
doorbell camera — it films the burglar, after they've walked in.

**Citations:** [cfpb-freeze], [fcra-1681c1], [cfpb-idtheft], [cfpb-monitoring]

**Diagram concept:** A front door shown twice, side by side — once with a
deadbolt (freeze) and once with only a doorbell camera recording a burglar
walking through (monitoring).

## 4. Unfreezing ("thawing") — what you'll need, and when

You'll need to lift your freeze whenever someone must check your credit file
to say yes to you: a new loan or credit card, and often a cell phone plan or
an apartment, since phone companies and landlords use reporting companies
too [cfpb-companies].

Good news: unlocking is free, every time, forever [cfpb-freeze]. If you ask
online or by phone, the bureau must lift the freeze within one hour. By mail,
within three business days [fcra-1681c1] [cfpb-freeze]. You can lift it just
for a window of time — say, one week while a car dealer runs your
application — and it refreezes on its own [cfpb-freeze].

One habit matters: when you place a freeze, each bureau gives you a PIN
(a personal code) or has you set up an account password. That's your key.
Write it down and keep it somewhere safe at home — you'll need it to thaw.
Tip: before you apply for anything, ask which bureau the company checks, so
you only have to thaw that one.

**Citations:** [cfpb-freeze], [fcra-1681c1], [cfpb-companies]

**Diagram concept:** A padlock on a timeline — unlocked for a labeled
"car loan week," then snapping shut again by itself.

## 5. Why children are prime targets

A child usually has no credit file at all — and that's exactly what a thief
wants. The government puts it plainly: thieves like children's Social
Security numbers "because children have no credit blemishes and because the
fraudulent activity may go unchecked for years" [cfpb-child-report]. Nobody
runs a credit check on a 7-year-old. The fraud can sit and grow until your
kid turns 18 and applies for their first card or student loan — and gets
denied for debts they never knew existed.

The fix: a "protected consumer" freeze. Federal law lets a parent or
guardian freeze a child under 16 — and if the child has no file yet, the
bureau creates one just so it can be frozen shut [fcra-1681c1]
[cfpb-blog-2018]. This is done by mail, with paper proof: Equifax's own form
asks for copies of your ID, the child's birth certificate, and the child's
Social Security card, mailed in [equifax-minor-form]. Experian's process is
mail-in with the same kinds of documents [experian-minor]. It's free
[equifax-minor-form].

**Citations:** [cfpb-child-report], [fcra-1681c1], [cfpb-blog-2018],
[equifax-minor-form], [experian-minor]

**Diagram concept:** A timeline from age 5 to age 18 showing fraud quietly
piling up unseen, ending at a stamped "DENIED" first loan application — versus
a frozen branch of the timeline where nothing accumulates.

## 6. Teens 16 and 17: they do it themselves

The child ("protected consumer") process has an age limit written into the
law: it covers kids **under 16** [fcra-1681c1]. So parents cannot use the
mail-in child forms for a 16- or 17-year-old.

Instead, a 16- or 17-year-old requests their own **standard** freeze — the
same kind an adult gets. Per Equifax, minors who are 16 or 17 may request
their own security freeze by phone or by mail, but must be 18 to open the
online account that manages a freeze on the web
[equifax-minor-help — **UNVERIFIED**: page blocked our reader; wording
confirmed only through a search preview. Experian's and TransUnion's exact
rules for 16–17-year-olds were not confirmed at all.]

Practical note for parents: the teen makes the request, but you can sit with
them, dial together, and help fill the envelope. Have their Social Security
card and birth certificate handy, and make sure they keep the PIN — it's
theirs now.

**Citations:** [fcra-1681c1], [equifax-minor-help] (UNVERIFIED — see flag)

**Diagram concept:** Three age doors in a row — "Under 16: parent mails the
form," "16–17: teen calls or mails, themselves," "18+: anyone, online in
minutes."

## 7. Why there are more than 3 places to freeze

The big three bureaus are not the only companies keeping files on your
family. The government keeps a public list of dozens of "specialty" reporting
companies — separate outfits that track narrower slices of your life: opening
bank accounts, utility and phone service, apartment rentals, and short-term
"payday"-style loans (the government files those under "low-income and
subprime") [cfpb-companies].

Here's the catch: freezing Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion does nothing at
these other companies. Each keeps its own file, so each needs its own freeze.
Examples from the government's list: Innovis (a fourth general bureau), NCTUE
(used for utilities and phone service), and LexisNexis (insurance records) —
all offer freezes [cfpb-companies]. ChexSystems, which banks check before
letting you open a checking account, offers a security freeze through its own
site [chexsystems — **UNVERIFIED** whether it is free; we confirmed the
freeze exists but did not read the terms].

FrostFile's checklist covers these extra stops so a thief can't just walk
around the block to an unlocked door.

**Citations:** [cfpb-companies], [chexsystems] (partly UNVERIFIED — see flag)

**Diagram concept:** A house with the front door bolted (big three) but a
side door, garage door, and cellar hatch still open, each labeled with a

## 8. "Is this legal? Why is it free?"

Completely legal — in fact, it's your federal right, and Congress is why it
costs nothing.

In May 2018, after the huge Equifax breach, Congress passed the **Economic
Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act** (Public Law
115-174). Its Section 301 is titled "Protecting consumers' credit"
[plaw-115-174]. Starting September 21, 2018, that law made placing a freeze,
lifting it, and placing it again **free** at every nationwide bureau, for
everyone, in every state — no fee, ever [cfpb-blog-2018]. Before this law,
many states let bureaus charge for each freeze and each thaw.

The rules now live in the Fair Credit Reporting Act (the main federal law
about credit files). It says freezes are "free of charge," sets the deadlines
bureaus must meet, and created the free child freeze too [fcra-1681c1].

So no — this is not a loophole, a trick, or a service you'll be billed for
later. It's a right you already own. FrostFile just helps you use it.

**Citations:** [plaw-115-174], [cfpb-blog-2018], [fcra-1681c1]

**Diagram concept:** A simple "your rights" card, styled like a library card,
stamped "FREE — Federal law, Sept 21, 2018," with the three bureau names as
checkboxes.
